## Proposed Additions

### To "Factors Shaping Complementarity" → "Team composition and size" bullet (append)

Dell'Acqua et al.'s (2026) P&G field experiment supplies direct field evidence for the diminishing-returns intuition: reading their conditions as sequential team expansion (individual → dyad → triad-with-AI), the *first* teammate added — whether a human or an AI — delivered most of the average quality gain, while the *second* addition yielded little further average improvement but disproportionately raised the odds of a top-decile breakthrough (Team+AI was ~3× as likely as a solo worker to land in the top decile). This suggests optimal configurations depend more on capturing the essential functional expertise than on raw head count — and that the marginal teammate's value shows up in the *tail* (exceptional outcomes) rather than the mean.

### To "Factors Shaping Complementarity" → "Trust calibration" bullet (append)

A second field caution from the same study bears on the framework's *interrogation* principle: AI-assisted workers were measurably worse than human teams at selecting their own best idea, which the authors attribute partly to AI's tendency to *affirm* rather than introduce dissent — "the validating nature of AI feedback may itself erode critical engagement" [p.16]. The framework's reliance on humans actively interrogating AI is therefore not just good practice but a hedge against a measured failure mode: absent deliberate friction, calibrated trust drifts toward comfortable over-acceptance at exactly the evaluative step where human judgment matters most.
